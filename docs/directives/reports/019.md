All four acceptance checks pass. Three findings:

1. **D1 (rate).** Without loss, x265 is no cheaper than coder Q on the same voxel counts:
   - the bits ratio x265 / coder Q is 0.92 to 1.02 at slices of a sixth of a window or longer, and 1.06 to
     1.18 at slices of 1/32 and 1/64;
   - P frames are coded mostly as merge on eTraM (66–95% of the coding units) and mostly as inter on DSEC
     (68–87%).

   The QP ladder of 019 (12 to 28) never comes below about 0.3 bits per event. So Table 3 is empty under
   0.5 bits per event, and most Fsnr targets of Table 1 fall outside the ladder.
2. **D2 (representation).** With 1 px and at least 3 slices per window, the counts lose nothing for the
   box tasks (box F 1.00, no empty box). With one slice per window the box F falls to 0.33 and the motion
   error rises to 24–28 px. At 2 px the motion error is 1.2 to 8 px, against 0 to 1.5 px at 1 px.
3. **D3 (model).** The oracle shift follows Eq. (binfidmoved) within 0.005 wherever the equation applies
   (Δt ≤ τ/2). On the same scenes, x265 lossless spends a growing number of P-frame bits as Δt falls below τ:
   about 0.05–0.8 bits per event at Δt/τ ≥ 4, rising to 8–14 at Δt/τ ≤ 2.

## 8. Deviations and notes

1. **Restart to keep the summed fidelity terms.** `code_counts` returns each group's F and Fsnr but not the
   sums behind them, and Section 6 asks for sums over groups. The runner wraps `ec.hevcdiag.fidelities` to
   also return its summed terms (N, N2, M, Dsnr, Lsnr); F and Fsnr are unchanged. A first launch (`2b95d22`)
   was stopped after 15 minutes, before any D1 group finished. Its outputs were deleted, along with the
   orphaned x265 processes. Every task of the reported run is at `efd6df6`. The determinism check was run
   again after the restart.
2. **Table 3** is computed on the frontier (median bits and scores over recordings per s, k and QP), not
   per recording.
3. **The Fsnr targets of Table 1** are interpolated per recording, between the first two consecutive QPs
   (in QP order) that bracket the target.
4. **Task pooling.** The task scores of a recording pool the boxes, tracks and squares of its groups, as in
   018.
5. **`model.json`** keeps the per-mode totals of each scene and grid (bits, CPU, I and P bits, mean
   P-frame shares, F, Fsnr). It does not keep x265's per-frame rows (up to 256 per mode), which are on the
   host in the task outputs.
6. **Entering.** Recordings with fewer D1 groups than the entering rule (2 on eTraM) are left out of the D1
   tables: 13 of 55 day and 11 of 45 night recordings have a single D1 group.

## 9. Questions for the director

1. **The QP ladder** (12 to 28) is dense where one-event voxels vanish, but its cheapest point costs 0.2 to
   1 bit per event. Table 3's budgets of 0.05 to 0.5 bits are therefore empty, and most Fsnr targets fall
   outside the ladder. Should the ladder be extended toward QP 46, as in 018, to fill them?
2. **Gate G0.** Which of the three costs justifies a new coder is the author's call. In short:
   - frames cost 6–18% more than a voxel list at fine slices (D1);
   - one slice per window loses two thirds of the box fidelity before any coding (D2);
   - x265 lossless needs 5–14 P-frame bits per event once Δt ≤ τ, where the oracle shift itself falls to
     Eq. (binfidmoved) (D3).
