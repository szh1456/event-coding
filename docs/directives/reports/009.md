## 6. Deviations and incidents

1. **Cost over the limit after the fact.** The spacing was chosen once from the projection:
   149.2 CPU-hours at S = 60, under 150 (295.9 at S = 30). The run took 169.9 CPU-hours, 14%
   more. Directive 009 has no rule for this case. As in directive 007, Section 15, the run was let
   finish, and both numbers are reported.
2. **Memory.** The mandated reader holds about 44 bytes per event during a read. The largest file
   (`zurich_city_02_c`, 3.31 billion events) peaks near 145 GB. A first census with three workers
   left 6 GB free on `cnt` when two reads (116 and 121 GB) coincided. It was stopped after six
   minutes, before it had written anything, and rerun with one worker. From `22563b1` on, the
   runner reads one recording at a time. A Stage R task keeps only the events of the windows its
   groups use, which `evaluate_group` reads alone, so results are unchanged. In Stage R a guard
   capped new launches at 12 workers when available memory fell below 40 GB, which happened four
   times. Memory was not exhausted again.
3. **hdf5plugin** was needed and installed (D-009-1), in the project's private package directory,
   not system-wide.
4. **The receiver** was one separate process for all 41 sequences, about 7.8 hours. It received the
   stream files, and read the events only to score.
5. **Choices where the directive is silent:** Stage R ran in tasks of 12 consecutive groups of one
   sequence. Ratios are formed per sequence from its sums, then the median is taken. Table 10 pools
   the blocks of all groups of all sequences from the streams `affine_Q_K11`.

## 7. Questions for the director

1. **Cost.** The run exceeded the 150-hour limit by 14% after a projection just under it. Should
   later directives on DSEC carry a margin, or a rule for an overrun?
2. **`interlaken_`.** In this location the gain at K = 4 is 1.204, under the threshold of 1.25. The
   rule reads stratum `all` (and `zurich_city_` at 1.313 also passes). Should the paper state the
   locations?
3. **Static gain.** Repeating the key events gains 0.866 at K = 4, above the predicted range, and
   1.59 at K = 11. The ratio to it (1.500 at K = 4) is lower than on eTraM by day (2.574). Should the
   paper discuss why repetition does better on this dataset? This report does not test an explanation.
