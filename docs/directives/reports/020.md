**Answer.** On recordings, the fidelity F of the causal prediction does **not** follow Eq. (binfidmoved) as
Δt/τ falls, with τ measured pixel by pixel:

- **Small r = Δt/τ.** F stays far above the equation. At 1 px and 1/32 window, F is 0.10 (DSEC), 0.18 (day)
  and 0.11 (night) at r ≈ 1/128, where the equation gives 0.009. At 1/256 window and r < 1/128 it is 0.016
  to 0.022, against at most 0.007.
- **Near r = 1/4 to 1/2.** F falls below the equation: 0.25 against 0.36–0.42 on DSEC at 1/32 window.
- **Within one grid**, F rises only about 3-fold while r spans a factor of 180. **Across grids**, F falls
  roughly with Δt (about halving per halving of the slice), whatever the per-pixel τ.
- **By speed.** Only the slow blocks (|d| ≤ 1 px per window) come close to tracking r. At 1/32 window F is
  0.05, 0.19, 0.46 and 0.81 by day at r = 1/128, 1/8, 1/2 and 2. Blocks moving 6 px per window or more stay
  near 0.2 at every r.

**F against the null.** F sits above the null F0, which has the right pixels and random times, on every
grid finer than one slice per window:
- at 3 to 12 slices per window, by +0.05 to +0.19;
- at 32 to 256 slices, by about a factor of two (for example 0.093 against 0.048 on DSEC at 1/64 window,
  r ≈ 0.044);
- with one slice per window, F = F0 by construction.

So the prediction carries timing information beyond the pixels, but much less than the model's
per-pixel law implies at small r, and it does not vanish there.

## 9. Questions for the director

1. **What F tracks instead.** On recordings, F follows the slice length Δt more than the per-pixel τ. It
   stays well above Eq. (binfidmoved) at small r and below it near r = 1/2. Only blocks that barely move
   behave like the model. Should the bridge between the model and the recordings be stated in terms of Δt
   and speed, rather than the per-pixel r?
2. **Search radius.** A fifth to a quarter of the blocks hit the search radius, and those blocks hold most
   of the fast events. Should a later run widen R before the speed classes are interpreted?
