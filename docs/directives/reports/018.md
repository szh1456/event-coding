All five acceptance checks pass.

- **HEVC is the strongest family on every stratum.** The video coder (x265 on the voxel counts of the
  working grid) reaches REMAKE's event fidelity F with 0.12 to 0.38 times REMAKE's bits:
  - day: 0.15 at K = 4 and 0.21 at K = 11;
  - night: 0.34 and 0.38;
  - DSEC: 0.13 and 0.12.
- **The subset families do not.** Against them, REMAKE keeps the gains of the earlier reports.
- **Equal bits.** At REMAKE's bits, HEVC scores higher than REMAKE on all four fidelities and on most task
  scores.
- **Which fidelity predicts the tasks.** Among the four candidates, only Fsnr agrees in sign with every
  task score on eTraM. For the box fidelity it agrees about as well as F.

## 1. What ran

All runs were on `cnt` (`cnt-js01`), under `nice -n 19` with one BLAS thread. No companion-project job
was running.

1. **Normative files.** Appendices A and B were written to `ec/families.py` and
   `tests/test_families_gate.py`. Their SHA-256 match Section 10 (`328c437`).
2. **ffmpeg (D-018-2).** The host already carries the Ubuntu build, ffmpeg 4.4.2-0ubuntu0.22.04.1 with
   libx265 (`libx265-199` 3.5-2), at `/usr/bin/ffmpeg`. It was used as it is; nothing was installed. The
   director's tests used ffmpeg 6.1.1. Appendix B's tests, including the HEVC ones, pass with this build.
3. **Section 5** (`scripts/fm_run.py run`, 11:40 on 2026-10-13 to 03:59 UTC on 2026-10-14, 26 workers).
   - Every group of directive 016's Stage R: 4,992 eTraM and 1,189 DSEC, 6,181 in all.
   - For each, `ec.families.evaluate_group(ev, w, W, H, g["records"], g, boxes=boxes, squares=squares,
     workdir=...)` with all 43 schemes.
   - The boxes come from `ec.regions.boxes_at` with the tracks and the DSEC time base of directive 017;
     the squares from `fb_run.squares_for` of window w + 2.
   - Events: eTraM was read with `bk_run.read`, once per task of 6 groups. DSEC came from the caches that
     directive 017 wrote while reading each sequence once (Section 6, item 2).
4. **Acceptance check 3** (`fm_run.py determinism`), Section 2.

## 8. Deviations and notes

1. **ffmpeg version.** The host's ffmpeg 4.4.2 with x265 3.5 was used instead of an installed build. The
   director tested with ffmpeg 6.1.1, and the x265 version determines the HEVC streams.
2. **DSEC events from the cache of directive 017.** Each group's events of `[(w − 3) T0, (w + 11) T0)` were
   cached while 017 read each sequence once under the read lock, and the range of Section 4 was cut from
   them. No DSEC event file was read for this directive. The groups' true-event counts equal those of the
   kept REMAKE groups (`remake_equals_kept` checks the key window and the matches of every window).
3. **eTraM reads.** At most six at once, as in report 016 (its Section 7, item 1).
4. **Instrumentation.** `ec.families.window_sums` was wrapped from the runner to add `n_enter` and
   `n_blocks`, the counts behind Table 4's share of blocks entering Fsnr. Its other outputs are unchanged.
   Record decoding went through `ec.codec.use_fast_decoders`.
5. **Aggregation.**
   - Table 1 uses each recording's own REMAKE `b_direct`.
   - Table 2 interpolates within each family's points, sorted by b.
   - Table 3 drops the schemes with a NaN score per recording, and also NaN values of τ (fewer than two
     schemes).
   - Table 4's `median_param_sei_share` is per recording, from sums.
6. **Box sizes.** The task scores by box size (50–199, 200–999, ≥ 1000 events) are in
   `per_recording.json`, under `tasks/box_<size>_*`.

## 9. Questions for the director

1. HEVC on the counts of the working grid reaches REMAKE's fidelity with a seventh to a third of its
   bits, and at REMAKE's bits it scores higher on most tasks. The fidelity is measured on the very grid
   HEVC codes, while REMAKE codes key events at one pixel and moves them. Should the comparison also be
   made on a finer grid, for example (1, 3), where HEVC on 2-pixel counts cannot be exact?
2. On DSEC, the background estimate takes the high-rate root in 58% of the groups, and θ has a median of
   250 events per block and window. Is that the intended regime for Fsnr on a moving camera?
