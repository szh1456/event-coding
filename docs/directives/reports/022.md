**The cause of check 2's mismatch.** I reproduced two of the groups and found the pixel in each:

- `interlaken_00_g`, j = 25: two null events at 51,039,739 and 51,063,694 µs, with τ_i = 9,582 µs.
- `interlaken_00_d`, j = 10: two predicted events at 21,040,868 and 21,043,991 µs, with τ_i = 2,082 µs.

In each, the two times differ by exactly 2.5 τ_i or 1.5 τ_i, and τ_i is even. Their phases therefore differ
by exactly one half (0.627 against 0.127, and 0.085 against 0.585). The two unit vectors cancel to exactly
`0j`, and `pixel_phases` keeps a pixel only if `abs(z) > 0`. So the pixel drops from the statistic of the
set where the cancellation happens (`null` in the first group, `pred` in the second) and stays in the
other. This is the code of Appendix A as written, applied to integer microsecond times; nothing was
changed. The effect on any reported mean is below 1e-6.

## 5. Questions for the director

1. **Phase reading.** By the rule of Section 6, the phase test reads independent by day and partial at
   night and on DSEC. The partial share comes from pixels with τ_i of 4 to 16 ms, the band of mains
   flicker beyond the ±2% tolerance. Below 4 ms the phase is uniform on every stratum. Should the flicker
   class be widened, or the reading be stated for τ_i < 4 ms?
2. **Exact cancellation.** `pixel_phases` drops a pixel whose summed phase vector is exactly 0. Should
   such a pixel count on both sides, for example by dropping it from `pred` and `null` together? It affects
